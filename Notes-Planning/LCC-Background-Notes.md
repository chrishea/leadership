Reference material consolidated from the May 2025, June 2025, and master planning notes, plus the 2025 cohort summary. Use this as the historical / civic-context backstop for the program. **For the current 2026 plan, see `LeadershipCloudcroft-master-notes.md` — that file wins on any conflict.**

## Program Origins & Purpose

Leadership Cloudcroft was created to address a structural gap in village life: no formal pipeline existed to identify and prepare the volunteers who actually run a small mountain town. The program builds engaged civic leaders by exposing them to how the village operates, the people who run it, and the issues shaping its future.

Year one launched in 2025 with a six-class format (June–October). The program is profiled in the Cloudcroft Reader's launch piece ([Developing Cloudcroft Volunteer Leaders](https://www.cloudcroftreader.com/p/developing-cloudcroft-volunteer-leaders), May 5, 2025).

## Cloudcroft Civic Context

### Tourism & Economic Development

- Significant tourism gap January through May; businesses struggle in winter months with low visitor numbers and no scheduled events.
- Existing draws like "Wine and Pines" operate only in peak season.
- Need for off-season strategies surfaced repeatedly in early planning.

### Local Government Structure

- Village relies heavily on professional support:
  - **Zach Cook** — Village Attorney; consulted almost daily. Previous $500/month arrangement proved inadequate.
  - **Council of Governments (COG)** — provides critical municipal support.
  - **Debbie Lee** — key resource for municipal guidance.
  - **Jolene** — Village Clerk; the clerk position operates at the same effective level as the mayor.
- **Karen Gutierrez** manages financial systems effectively.

### Chamber of Commerce Issues

- Operating with a $25/year lease, which conflicts with anti-donation law.
- Market rate for the space is approximately $240 per square foot annually.
- Lodgers tax management needs reassessment; historical knowledge is lost between administrations.
- An RFP system has been floated as an alternative funding distribution method.
- Audit findings have surfaced ongoing compliance issues.

### Village Development Snapshot

- Building activity is minimal: 3 houses completed last year, 4 projected this year.
- Contractor availability is a major constraint (Dan Hughes booked four years out).
- Planning & Zoning modernization underway — Rick Donnelly working on updated application processes.
- Fire risk considerations are now factoring into remodeling decisions.

## Year One (2025) — Cohort & Format

### The Cohort

- 14 confirmed inaugural participants.
- Roughly 50% dropout rate, attributed largely to scheduling conflicts — the central driver for the year-two redesign.

### Format & Logistics

- Six class days, June through October 2025.
- Venue priority: Lodge (preferred) → Council Chamber (backup) → Library (final option).
- Refreshments provided (changed from "lunch provided" because of catering constraints).
- Morning sessions with afternoon breaks.
- Slack used for program communications (free tier to start; potential ~$1,000/year upgrade if 90-day retention becomes limiting).
- Slide decks built with Gamma.ai; custom bear illustrations replaced stock imagery for distinctiveness.

## Leadership Pipeline Goals

The program's deeper purpose is to convert engaged residents into civic role-holders.

- **Target audience for civic engagement:** young residents (18+).
- **Internship pipelines into village departments:**
  - Water clerk position
  - Court system roles
  - Administrative positions
- **Precedents to point to:**
  - **Lucas Medina** — Otero County Electric internship
  - **Jenny Turie** — started in courts, became clerk
